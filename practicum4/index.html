Practicum 4

Camera Playground

Practicum 3 moved flat shapes with a 3 by 3 matrix. This one adds the rest of the journey. A cube lives in local space, a model matrix spins it, a view matrix places the camera, and a projection matrix flattens everything for the screen. Every canvas below runs that full chain live.

The Stage

This is the main bench. One cube spins on its own while you fly the camera around it. The cube never leaves the origin. Only the view and projection change as you move, and the HUD reports every value back each frame.

Arrow keys slide the camera sideways and up and down. W moves it closer and S pulls it back. Bracket keys shrink and widen the view. Digits 1 2 and 3 jump to fixed FOV values. P flips projection and D flips depth and N steps the clip preset and O toggles orbit and T steps the target and PageUp and PageDown change height and R resets and Space pauses.

Live data

How it works

  • Model spins the cube while view and projection stay separate uniforms.
  • Held keys move the camera every frame with delta time.
  • Single press keys flip one setting each and never repeat.

Try this

  • Press D and watch the back faces punch through the front.
  • Press P and compare how depth feels in each projection.
  • Press M to log the live matrices to the console.
LAB A

Camera presets

Same cube and same target, three camera seats. The cube never moves in the world. Only the view matrix changes, and the picture follows it.

camera

Seats are (0,0,4) then (2,0,4) then (0,2,4). The target stays at the origin.

Live data

How it works

  • Moving right turns into looking left from the new seat.
  • Moving up looks down at the cube from above.
  • View equals the inverse of the camera transform.
LAB B

Target plus up

The camera stays parked while its gaze wanders. Shifting the target steers the view direction, and tilting the up vector rolls the picture.

target

Targets are origin then (1,0,0) then (minus 1,0,0). The up toggle rolls the frame sideways.

Live data

How it works

  • Forward equals target minus position, normalized.
  • Right comes from crossing forward with up.
  • Position plus target alone leave roll undecided until up joins.
LAB C

Split projection

Two views of the same moment. The left half uses perspective and the right half uses orthographic, with one shared model and camera state.

split

Just watch. Both halves spin together and only the projection differs.

Live data

How it works

  • Perspective shrinks far geometry and feels natural.
  • Orthographic keeps sizes stable and feels technical.
  • Same matrices in, two projections out.
LAB D

Depth trio

Three cubes at three depths share one screen. With depth on, the near cube correctly hides the far ones. With depth off, draw order decides and the picture lies.

depth

Cubes sit at z zero and minus 1.5 and plus 1.5. The clip button steps the same near and far presets as the stage.

Live data

How it works

  • Depth buffer keeps the closest fragment per pixel.
  • Without it the last drawn triangle wins, near or far.
  • Tight clips slice cubes while loose clips waste precision.
LAB E

FOV plus aspect

The lens bench. Narrow FOV zooms in and wide FOV stretches the edges. The aspect toggle swaps the canvas shape to prove the projection must follow the real viewport.

fov

FOV steps through 35 then 60 then 90 degrees. Aspect swaps square and wide canvas shapes.

Live data

How it works

  • Small FOV narrows the view and reads as zoom.
  • Large FOV widens the view and bends the edges.
  • Wrong aspect stretches the cube like a bad projector.
